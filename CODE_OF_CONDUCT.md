# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want contributing to the DDB Skillhub to be a straightforward, welcoming
experience for anyone, regardless of experience level, background, identity, or
which agent runtime they happen to prefer.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be technical, not personal.** Critique the skill, the trigger phrasing, the
  safety properties. Never the person who wrote it.
- **Assume good faith.** A skill that duplicates yours, or a review that asks
  for a rewrite, is normal collaboration rather than an attack.
- **Give actionable reviews.** "This won't trigger" is not a review. "The
  description needs the words a user would say — try *insert this into a
  spreadsheet*" is.
- **Respect scope.** A skill's author decides what their skill covers. Offer
  improvements; do not repurpose someone's skill for a different job.
- **Credit sources.** If you adapt a skill from elsewhere, say so and respect
  its license.
- **Keep it public.** Design decisions happen in issues and pull requests so
  everyone can follow them.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or discriminatory language of any kind.
- Sexualised language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Submitting skills that are deliberately malicious, deceptive, or designed to
  exfiltrate data, weaken safety controls, or harm a user's system.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or badgering reviewers for a merge.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a problem in good faith.

## Scope

This code applies in repository issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and any space where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour to the maintainers by opening a confidential
report through the repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability** form, or
by contacting a maintainer directly on GitHub. Reports are handled privately.

Maintainers will acknowledge a report within a few days and will explain the
action taken. Maintainers who are themselves the subject of a report are
excluded from handling it.

## Consequences

Depending on severity, maintainers may:

1. ask for a change in behaviour and take no further action,
2. remove or edit the offending content with a warning,
3. temporarily restrict participation,
4. permanently ban the account from the project.
